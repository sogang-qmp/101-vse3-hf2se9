Motivation — a single-molecule junction grown inside a nanotube

Collaboration experiment (manuscript in preparation) — this page records the experimental findings that motivate the calculation, not our results. Formal citation goes to References on publication.

1. Background — the missing selenium member

Transition-metal trichalcogenides (TMTCs, MX₃) are quasi-1D crystals of weakly coupled atomic chains. In the single-chain limit the tellurium members (VTe₃, NbTe₃) are known to take trigonal antiprismatic (TAP) coordination — but the selenium counterpart VSe₃ had never been isolated as a single chain.

2. The experiment

  • Single-chain VSe₃ realized inside carbon nanotubes. Nanotube-confined growth stabilizes the otherwise inaccessible isolated chain.
  • STEM identifies it as trigonal prismatic (TP) — like NbSe₃, unlike the TAP tellurides. This completes the missing Se member of the group-V TMTC single-chain family and establishes a chalcogen-dependent coordination trend under 1D confinement (DFT on local coordination units confirms the TP preference — 01. VSe₃ TP vs TAP).
  • Beyond the pristine chain: the confined channel also shows atomic-scale Hf incorporation (Z-contrast ADF-STEM) and the formation of Se-rich molecular species — in particular a Hf₂Se₉ single molecule sitting between two VSe₃ single chains: a naturally formed 1D heterojunction at the single-chain limit (manuscript Fig. 5, experimental + simulated STEM + atomic model).

3. Our question

That Figure-5 motif — metal chain / single molecule / metal chain — is a molecular junction the experiment can grow but not measure electrically. This project computes its conductance from first principles: does current flow through the Hf₂Se₉ molecule, through which orbital, and where do its levels really sit?

4. From the experiment to the results
